A video editor with an engine under it.

Sparkle is a desktop application for people who cut for a living. GPU rendering, AI models that run on your own machine, hardware encoding that adapts to your silicon — and an assistant that never touches your cut without being asked.

Most of an edit is not editing

Before anybody makes a creative decision, somebody watches the footage end to end, marks the scene changes, groups the takes, cuts the silences, types the subtitles, and gets twelve clips from three cameras to agree on what colour the evening was.

Days of it, before the first real decision. None of it the work anyone trained for.

Tools that promise to fix this usually fix it by taking the decisions: hand over the footage, receive an edit, hope you agree. Sparkle goes the other way.

The machine takes the labour. You keep the cut.

Thousands of frames, one preview

Most transitions are shaders that run on the GPU while you scrub — a plain fade, and anything sitting next to a still, take a simpler path. The export runs none of those shaders. It uses the encoder's own transition set, where about ten of ours have a match by name and the rest are approximated to the nearest look it has. The preview is a close guide to the file rather than a promise of it, and transitions are where the two come apart.

Heavy footage gets proxies and a scrub path built for long timelines, which is the difference between an editor you can think in and one you wait for.

Most of the AI runs on your machine

Background removal, subject segmentation and depth estimation run as local models on your own hardware. An afternoon of rushes does not have to be uploaded to become useful, and for this work footage under embargo stays under embargo.

Speech to text can run locally too, on a Whisper model the editor fetches once and then runs without a network. Name that model and the audio stays on the machine. Leave the engine on Auto and it goes to a cloud engine whenever one is configured, at any length — so where your audio goes is a setting, and it is one worth setting.

Which settings keep audio on your machine

Masks track across frames using optical flow rather than being redrawn shot by shot. Captions arrive with word-level timing, so they can be styled, re-cut and exported to the formats the rest of the industry expects.

The heavier generative work needs the cloud, and that part is metered where you can see it. Every account holds a balance of credits; an operation reserves what it might cost before it starts and settles for what it actually cost. Nobody discovers a bill at the end of the month.

What it will not do

It does not take the edit off you. The assistant does not touch a cut it was not asked to touch, and it cannot do anything the editor itself cannot do — which is the reason a single undo takes any of it back.

Export that talks to your hardware

Encoding uses whatever accelerator it finds — NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync, AMD, Apple VideoToolbox — and drops to x264, x265 or SVT-AV1 in software when there is nothing to talk to. H.264, HEVC, AV1 and DNxHR for the edit suite, with HDR handled rather than flattened.

Colour goes through a 3D LUT pipeline, so a look built elsewhere arrives intact. Audio can be loudness-normalised on the way out — a measurement pass to a -14 LUFS master — because a delivery that fails loudness spec is a delivery you make twice. It is a checkbox rather than a default, so it is worth finding once.

Who it is for

Editors and small post-production teams — people who live in a timeline and notice every hour the software gives back. The desktop editor is free. Cloud AI is billed per member of your organisation, so three editors pay for three rather than a licence tier somebody invented. There is no idle rate: a member who never opens the editor costs the same as one who edits daily. The credit balance above is how the cloud work on an account is metered.

Where it is today

Sparkle is in private preview. Editor, cloud services, accounts and billing are built and running; access opens gradually, because software that handles other people's footage and other people's money deserves to be let out slowly. If you cut for a living and want in, write and say what you cut.
