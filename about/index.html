A technology company that ships.

Spinne Software makes its own software instead of taking contracts. It is one engineer in Rome, and the order of the work is deliberate: build the machinery once, then build the products that stand on it. The ambition is stated plainly — products that hold their own against the best in their category, built by a company small enough to finish them.

Why products rather than clients

Contract work pays for someone else's roadmap. Owning the product means the engineering compounds instead of being handed over — every system Sparkle needed, the next thing inherits.

It also means the decisions stay here. What gets built next is settled by what the work needs rather than by whichever customer is largest this quarter, and the parts the product depends on are owned rather than rented from somebody whose priorities can change without notice.

Independence is not a preference. It is the difference between choosing and being told.

One engineer, and what that buys

Nothing is lost in translation between the person who decided something and the person who built it, and every part of the system still has an author who can explain why it is the way it is. What it costs is parallelism: one thing at a time, finished.

The name

Spinne is German for spider. A web is one structure made of many threads, each doing a specific job, and it is repaired rather than rebuilt.

How the work is decided

A feature starts from something a person cannot do today, not from a model that was released last week. The technology is chosen after the problem, which is why some of it runs on the machine in front of you, some of it runs in a data centre, and some of it was left out because the honest version did not work yet.

What is wrong gets fixed rather than documented. A defect is not a known limitation, and a design that only holds if the user is careful is a design that does not hold.

What exists

Sparkle — a desktop video editor with GPU rendering, local AI for matting, segmentation and depth, hardware encoding across four vendor stacks, and the team accounts, per-seat subscriptions, metered credits and Italian electronic invoicing that a product of that size cannot ship without. In private preview at sparkle.software. What it does · how it is built.

The exact position

Diligence questions arrive eventually, so here are the answers in advance, including the ones that are no.

  • Security controls: implemented, not audited. Role-based access, tenant isolation with tests that prove it, append-only audit logs, second-person approval on sensitive administrative actions, GDPR retention and erasure across the schema, credential rotation with recorded evidence. All built, and we will walk anyone through any of it. There is no SOC 2 attestation and no ISO/IEC 27001 certificate.
  • Card data never reaches us. Payments go through Stripe. No card number touches a system we operate.
  • On GDPR we build the mechanism; a controller writes the policy. Retention and erasure implemented in the schema rather than described in a document.
  • We build on Claude, GPT-5 and Gemini for the assistant; Google Veo, Kling, MiniMax Hailuo and Luma Ray for video; Nano Banana, FLUX, Seedream and Ideogram for images; ElevenLabs for voice, sound and dubbing; Suno for music; Topaz for restoration. They are reached through fal.ai, OpenRouter, AI/ML API and Higgsfield; payments run through Stripe, and speech to text through Deepgram and Google Cloud. Integration only — not a partnership, endorsement or affiliation.
